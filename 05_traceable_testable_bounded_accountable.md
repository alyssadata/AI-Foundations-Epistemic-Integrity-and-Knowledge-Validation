# AI Foundations: Epistemic Integrity and Knowledge Validation

## 05 Traceable, Testable, Bounded, and Accountable

**An answer is not knowledge unless its source, method, validation, uncertainty, and responsibility remain intact.**

## Definition

**Traceable, testable, bounded, and accountable** are the four operational requirements a claim must satisfy before it may cross the claim-to-knowledge boundary.

These terms are not decorative.

They define whether a claim can carry epistemic weight.

A claim may move toward knowledge only when it remains:

- traceable to source
- testable through method and validation
- bounded by uncertainty
- accountable through responsibility

Together, these requirements preserve the claim chain from source through use.

## Core Rule

A claim may cross the claim-to-knowledge boundary only when it remains:

1. **Traceable**
2. **Testable**
3. **Bounded**
4. **Accountable**

When one requirement fails, the claim remains below the knowledge boundary.

## Relationship Between the Five Conditions and Four Requirements

The five knowledge conditions are:

- source
- method
- validation
- uncertainty
- responsibility

The four operational requirements are:

- traceable
- testable
- bounded
- accountable

These function together.

Source supports traceability.

Method supports examination.

Validation supports testability.

Uncertainty supports boundedness.

Responsibility supports accountability.

A claim cannot carry knowledge-bearing authority when these conditions separate from these requirements.

## Traceable

A claim is **traceable** when its origin, source chain, evidence path, and transformation history can be followed.

Traceability answers the question:

**Where did this claim come from, and how did it become this output?**

A traceable claim preserves:

- where the claim originated
- what source supports it
- whether the source directly supports it
- how the claim moved from source into answer
- what was quoted
- what was summarized
- what was inferred
- what was synthesized
- what was generated
- what transformations occurred
- where the source chain remains intact
- where the source chain breaks

Traceability keeps the claim connected to source.

## Traceability Failure

Traceability fails when the system cannot show where the claim came from or how it moved into output.

Traceability fails when:

- the source is missing
- the source chain is unclear
- the source does not support the claim
- the claim is detached from its origin
- citation appearance replaces source integrity
- source language is added after the answer is formed
- a generated answer is presented as though its source chain remained intact
- the path from source to claim cannot be reviewed

A claim that is not traceable remains below the knowledge boundary.

## Testable

A claim is **testable** when it can be examined against evidence, method, reality, reproducibility, or appropriate domain standards.

Testability answers the question:

**Can this claim be examined, challenged, measured, reproduced, reviewed, or validated?**

A testable claim preserves:

- what is being claimed
- what evidence would support it
- what evidence would challenge it
- what method applies
- how the claim can be checked
- how the claim can be reviewed
- what would change the claim
- what remains untested
- what validation standard is appropriate
- whether the claim is specific enough to evaluate

Testability keeps the claim open to validation.

## Testability Failure

Testability fails when the claim cannot be meaningfully examined.

Testability fails when:

- the claim is too vague
- the claim is too compressed
- the claim is purely asserted
- the claim depends on authority language
- the method is hidden
- the evidence cannot be examined
- the claim is framed beyond available support
- the claim cannot be challenged by any relevant method
- the conclusion is presented without a path for review

A claim that is not testable remains below the knowledge boundary.

## Bounded

A claim is **bounded** when its scope, limits, confidence, conditions, and uncertainty remain visible.

Boundedness answers the question:

**Where does this claim stop?**

A bounded claim preserves:

- what is known
- what is not known
- what is inferred
- what is assumed
- what remains uncertain
- what remains conditional
- what evidence supports it
- what evidence does not support it
- what confidence level is appropriate
- what conditions limit the claim
- where the claim stops

Boundedness keeps the claim from exceeding its evidence.

## Boundedness Failure

Boundedness fails when a claim presents more certainty, scope, or authority than its evidence supports.

Boundedness fails when:

- uncertainty is compressed
- confidence is presented as certainty
- inference is presented as fact
- possibility is presented as conclusion
- partial evidence is presented as complete knowledge
- the claim exceeds its source
- the conclusion exceeds its method
- limitations are removed for readability or persuasion
- unknowns are hidden behind institutional or authoritative language

A claim that is not bounded remains below the knowledge boundary.

## Accountable

A claim is **accountable** when responsibility remains attached to its production, validation, publication, use, decision, or consequence.

Accountability answers the question:

**Who stands behind this claim, its use, or its consequences?**

An accountable claim preserves:

- who produced the claim
- who selected the sources
- who validated the claim
- who published the claim
- who authorized use
- who applied the claim
- who is responsible for consequences
- what system carries responsibility
- what institution carries responsibility
- where responsibility cannot be assigned

Accountability keeps authority connected to consequence.

## Accountability Failure

Accountability fails when a claim becomes authority without a responsible actor, system, institution, validator, publisher, decision-maker, or use-context.

Accountability fails when:

- responsibility is unnamed
- responsibility is detached from use
- passive voice hides the actor
- automation hides the decision path
- institutional tone replaces accountable ownership
- model output is treated as authority without named responsibility
- a claim informs action without a responsible party
- consequences are separated from the claim’s use

A claim that is not accountable remains below the knowledge boundary.

## Four-Requirement Test

A claim may be evaluated through the four-requirement test:

1. **Can the claim be traced?**
2. **Can the claim be tested?**
3. **Can the claim be bounded?**
4. **Can the claim be held accountable?**

If the answer to any question is no, the claim remains below the knowledge boundary.

The claim may remain useful.

It may guide research.

It may identify a pattern.

It may form a hypothesis.

It may create a direction for review.

It may not carry knowledge-bearing authority.

## Claim Status Under the Four Requirements

A claim remains an examined claim when it is partially traceable, partially testable, partially bounded, or partially accountable.

A claim may gain epistemic weight as more of the four requirements become intact.

A claim crosses into knowledge only when all four requirements hold under review.

A claim may carry action-bearing authority only when accountability and validation meet the consequence threshold of the use-context.

## Knowledge-Bearing Authority

Knowledge-bearing authority requires the claim to remain traceable, testable, bounded, and accountable.

A claim with knowledge-bearing authority may inform understanding, research direction, publication, governance reasoning, institutional reasoning, or public knowledge.

Knowledge-bearing authority is not granted by:

- fluency
- polish
- repetition
- confidence
- convergence
- authority
- consensus
- institutional tone
- citation appearance

Knowledge-bearing authority is earned through intact validation.

## Action-Bearing Authority

Action-bearing authority carries a higher responsibility threshold.

A claim moves toward action-bearing authority when it supports decisions, interventions, policies, legal judgments, medical guidance, governance action, system behavior, or material consequences.

A claim must not carry action-bearing authority when it is untraceable, untested, unbounded, or unaccountable.

The stronger the consequence, the stronger the validation and responsibility requirements.

## Autonomous Research Agent Context

Autonomous research agents make these requirements necessary at scale.

As agents search, retrieve, compare, synthesize, infer, cite, write, plan, recommend, or act, they can produce output that appears complete before the claim chain is intact.

For that reason, every agentic output must preserve the four requirements:

- traceable source chain
- testable claim structure
- bounded uncertainty
- accountable use-context

An autonomous research agent may produce an answer.

It may identify convergence.

It may generate a synthesis.

It may form a hypothesis.

It may recommend action.

Its output remains below the knowledge boundary until it is traceable, testable, bounded, and accountable.

## Manufactured Compliance Risk

Manufactured compliance occurs when a system makes a claim appear traceable, testable, bounded, or accountable through presentation rather than preserved structure.

This may happen when the system uses:

- citation-like formatting without source integrity
- method-like explanation without reviewable method
- confidence scores without preserved uncertainty
- institutional language without responsibility
- summary compression that hides limits
- source references added after the answer is formed
- action recommendations without accountable use-context

Manufactured compliance creates the appearance of epistemic integrity without preserving the claim chain.

AI Foundations rejects manufactured compliance as a substitute for validation.

## Preservation Standard

A claim preserves the four requirements when:

- traceability remains connected to source
- testability remains connected to method and validation
- boundedness remains connected to uncertainty
- accountability remains connected to responsibility
- the claim stays within its evidence
- the claim status remains visible
- inference remains labeled
- synthesis preserves source status
- authority does not exceed validation
- action remains tied to accountable use

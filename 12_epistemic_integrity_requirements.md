# AI Foundations: Epistemic Integrity and Knowledge Validation

## 12 Epistemic Integrity Requirements

**An answer is not knowledge unless its source, method, validation, uncertainty, and responsibility remain intact.**

## Definition

**Epistemic integrity** is the preservation of a claim’s source, method, validation, uncertainty, and responsibility from production through use.

A system has epistemic integrity when it prevents answers, claims, syntheses, inferences, hypotheses, reports, recommendations, or actions from carrying more authority than their validation status supports.

Epistemic integrity preserves the difference between:

- answer and knowledge
- claim and validated claim
- synthesis and proof
- inference and evidence
- convergence and truth
- authority and validation
- consensus and truth
- confidence and certainty
- provenance and manufactured provenance
- knowledge-bearing authority and action-bearing authority

## Core Rule

A system preserves epistemic integrity when every claim remains in its correct epistemic status until its source, method, validation, uncertainty, and responsibility remain intact.

A system loses epistemic integrity when it allows a claim to become knowledge, authority, recommendation, or action before those conditions hold.

## Requirement 1: Preserve Claim Status

A system must preserve the status of each output.

The system must distinguish:

- answer
- claim
- synthesis
- inference
- hypothesis
- evidence
- validation report
- recommendation
- action proposal
- validated knowledge

The system must not allow a produced answer to appear as validated knowledge unless the claim has crossed the claim-to-knowledge boundary through validation.

## Requirement 2: Preserve Source Integrity

A system must preserve where each claim came from.

Source integrity requires that the system can show:

- the origin of the claim
- the source or evidence supporting the claim
- whether the source directly supports the claim
- whether the source is primary, secondary, interpreted, summarized, repeated, or generated
- whether the source chain remains attached
- whether the claim has changed from source to output
- where the source chain breaks

A claim without source integrity remains below the knowledge boundary.

## Requirement 3: Preserve Method Visibility

A system must preserve how each claim was produced, gathered, compared, inferred, synthesized, tested, validated, or applied.

Method visibility requires that the system can show:

- how sources were selected
- how sources were compared
- how evidence was evaluated
- how inference was produced
- how synthesis was formed
- how contradiction was handled
- how uncertainty was preserved
- how validation was attempted
- how conclusions were limited

A hidden method prevents the claim from being examined.

## Requirement 4: Preserve Validation Status

A system must preserve whether validation has occurred.

Validation status requires that the system can show:

- what exact claim was tested
- what evidence supports the claim
- what evidence challenges the claim
- what method applies
- what standard applies
- what has been tested
- what remains untested
- what conditions would change the claim
- what level of confidence is appropriate
- who is responsible if the claim is used

Validation is the gate between claim and knowledge.

## Requirement 5: Preserve Uncertainty

A system must preserve uncertainty.

Uncertainty preservation requires that the system can show:

- what is known
- what is not known
- what is inferred
- what is assumed
- what remains conditional
- what remains unresolved
- what evidence is missing
- what evidence conflicts
- what confidence level is appropriate
- where the claim stops

A claim loses boundary integrity when uncertainty is compressed into certainty.

## Requirement 6: Preserve Responsibility

A system must preserve responsibility.

Responsibility preservation requires that the system can show:

- who produced the claim
- who selected the sources
- who validated the claim
- who published the claim
- who authorized use
- who applied the claim
- who is responsible for consequences
- what system carries responsibility
- what institution carries responsibility
- where responsibility cannot be assigned

A claim may not carry action-bearing authority when responsibility is detached.

## Requirement 7: Preserve Traceability

A system must make claims traceable.

Traceability requires that the claim’s origin, source chain, evidence path, and transformation history can be followed.

A traceable claim shows:

- where the claim originated
- what source supports it
- how the claim moved from source into output
- what was quoted
- what was summarized
- what was inferred
- what was synthesized
- what was generated
- what transformations occurred
- where the source chain remains intact
- where the source chain breaks

A claim that cannot be traced cannot cross the knowledge boundary.

## Requirement 8: Preserve Testability

A system must make claims testable.

Testability requires that the claim can be examined against evidence, method, reality, reproducibility, or appropriate domain standards.

A testable claim shows:

- what is being claimed
- what evidence would support it
- what evidence would challenge it
- what method applies
- what standard applies
- how the claim can be checked
- how the claim can be reviewed
- how the claim can be challenged
- what would change the claim

A claim that cannot be tested remains below the knowledge boundary.

## Requirement 9: Preserve Boundedness

A system must make claims bounded.

Boundedness requires that the claim’s scope, limits, confidence, conditions, and uncertainty remain visible.

A bounded claim shows:

- what is known
- what is not known
- what is inferred
- what is assumed
- what is uncertain
- what is conditional
- what evidence supports it
- what evidence does not support it
- what confidence level is appropriate
- where the claim stops

A claim that exceeds its evidence remains below the knowledge boundary.

## Requirement 10: Preserve Accountability

A system must make claims accountable.

Accountability requires that responsibility remains attached to the claim’s production, validation, publication, use, decision, or consequence.

An accountable claim shows:

- who produced the claim
- who validated the claim
- who published the claim
- who authorized use
- who applied the claim
- who is responsible for consequences
- what system or institution carries responsibility
- where responsibility cannot be assigned

A claim that becomes authority without accountability remains below the knowledge boundary.

## Requirement 11: Preserve Convergence as Signal

A system must treat convergence as signal, not truth.

When multiple sources, systems, observations, reports, outputs, methods, or claims appear to align, the system must preserve:

- what exact claim appears to converge
- which sources support the claim
- whether the sources are independent
- whether the sources directly support the claim
- what method identified the convergence
- what evidence challenges the convergence
- what remains uncertain
- what has been validated
- what has not been validated
- who is responsible if the claim is used

Convergence may guide validation.

Convergence does not replace validation.

## Requirement 12: Preserve Authority as Signal

A system must treat authority as signal, not truth.

When an authority source, expert, institution, publication, dataset, method, or role appears to support a claim, the system must preserve:

- who or what the authority source is
- what claim the authority source supports
- whether the authority source directly supports the claim
- whether the authority source is relevant to the domain
- whether the claim exceeds the authority source
- what method produced or supports the claim
- what evidence challenges the claim
- what remains uncertain
- what has been validated
- who is responsible if the claim is used

Authority may guide validation.

Authority does not replace validation.

## Requirement 13: Preserve Consensus as Signal

A system must treat consensus as signal, not truth.

When a claim appears widely accepted, widely repeated, institutionally dominant, or field-supported, the system must preserve:

- what exact claim appears to have consensus
- where the consensus appears
- which sources support the claim
- whether the sources are independent
- what evidence challenges the consensus
- what minority evidence remains relevant
- what method identified the consensus
- what remains uncertain
- what has been validated
- who is responsible if the claim is used

Consensus may guide validation.

Consensus does not replace validation.

## Requirement 14: Preserve Validation as Gate

A system must preserve validation as the gate between claim and knowledge.

The system must not allow a claim to cross into knowledge-bearing authority through:

- fluency
- polish
- repetition
- confidence
- citation appearance
- institutional tone
- convergence
- authority
- consensus
- user preference
- action pressure

Validation is not decorative.

Validation is the gate.

## Requirement 15: Preserve Provenance

A system must preserve provenance through the claim chain.

Provenance preservation requires that the system maintains:

- source chain
- evidence path
- transformation history
- method visibility
- validation status
- uncertainty limits
- disagreement status
- inference status
- synthesis status
- responsibility attachment
- use-context
- boundary status

The claim must remain connected to its origin, process, validation, limits, and responsibility.

## Requirement 16: Reject Manufactured Provenance

A system must reject manufactured provenance.

Manufactured provenance occurs when output is produced first and later wrapped in source-like, citation-like, method-like, validation-like, or authority-like language as though the claim chain had remained intact.

A system must not:

- form conclusions first and source them later
- add citations after the answer is produced as validation
- use sources that do not support the claim
- hide source selection
- hide method steps
- hide uncertainty
- hide disagreement
- treat repeated output as confirmation
- treat authority as validation
- treat consensus as truth
- recommend action without responsibility

**Source cannot be retroactively manufactured.**

## Requirement 17: Preserve Boundary Status

A system must preserve the claim-to-knowledge boundary.

The system must keep each claim in its correct epistemic status:

- answer remains answer
- claim remains claim
- synthesis remains synthesis
- inference remains inference
- hypothesis remains hypothesis
- evidence remains evidence
- validation report remains validation report
- recommendation remains recommendation
- action proposal remains action proposal
- validated knowledge remains tied to validation scope

Boundary crossing must be earned through intact validation.

## Requirement 18: Preserve Scope

A system must preserve the scope of validation.

A claim may be validated for one domain, method, condition, or use-context and not another.

The system must show:

- where validation applies
- where validation stops
- what conditions limit the claim
- what use-context is supported
- what use-context is not supported
- whether the claim may carry knowledge-bearing authority
- whether the claim may carry action-bearing authority

Validation does not create universal authority.

## Requirement 19: Preserve Revision Path

A system must preserve the possibility of revision.

A validated claim may change when:

- new evidence appears
- better method appears
- source integrity is challenged
- uncertainty changes
- conditions change
- contradictions emerge
- responsibility shifts
- use-context changes
- the claim is applied beyond its validated scope

Epistemic integrity requires that knowledge remains reviewable.

## Requirement 20: Preserve Consequence Thresholds

A system must apply stronger requirements when a claim moves toward action-bearing authority.

The stronger the consequence, the stronger the gate.

Action-bearing authority requires:

- stronger validation
- clearer responsibility
- clearer use-context
- clearer uncertainty limits
- clearer consequence awareness
- appropriate human or institutional review
- accountable decision pathway

A claim must not support material action when validation is incomplete or responsibility is detached.

## Autonomous Research Agent Requirements

Autonomous research agents must satisfy all epistemic integrity requirements before their outputs may carry knowledge-bearing or action-bearing authority.

An autonomous research agent must preserve:

- source integrity
- method visibility
- validation status
- uncertainty limits
- responsibility attachment
- traceability
- testability
- boundedness
- accountability
- provenance
- boundary status
- consequence thresholds

Autonomy does not reduce the requirements.

Autonomy increases the need for the requirements.

## Failure Conditions

A system fails epistemic integrity when:

- source is missing
- source does not support the claim
- method is hidden
- validation is incomplete
- uncertainty is compressed
- responsibility is detached
- the claim cannot be traced
- the claim cannot be tested
- the claim exceeds its evidence
- the claim becomes authority without accountability
- convergence is treated as truth
- authority is treated as validation
- consensus is treated as proof
- citations are used as decoration
- provenance is manufactured after output
- action is proposed without consequence responsibility

Failure does not always make the output useless.

Failure keeps the output below knowledge-bearing authority.

## Preservation Conditions

A system preserves epistemic integrity when:

- source remains attached
- method remains visible
- validation remains named
- uncertainty remains present
- responsibility remains attached
- claim status remains visible
- inference remains labeled
- synthesis preserves source status
- convergence remains signal
- authority remains signal
- consensus remains signal
- validation remains gate
- provenance remains preserved
- recommendations stay within validation
- action remains accountable

Preservation allows a claim to move toward epistemic weight.

## Knowledge-Bearing Authority

A claim may carry knowledge-bearing authority only when it remains traceable, testable, bounded, and accountable through intact validation.

Knowledge-bearing authority may inform:

- understanding
- research direction
- publication
- governance reasoning
- institutional reasoning
- public knowledge

Knowledge-bearing authority is earned through preserved epistemic integrity.

It is not granted by presentation.

## Action-Bearing Authority

A claim may carry action-bearing authority only when validation and responsibility meet the consequence threshold of the use-context.

Action-bearing authority may inform:

- decisions
- interventions
- policies
- legal judgments
- medical guidance
- governance action
- system behavior
- material consequences

Action-bearing authority requires stronger accountability because the claim is connected to consequence.

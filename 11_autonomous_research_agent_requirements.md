# AI Foundations: Epistemic Integrity and Knowledge Validation

## 11 Autonomous Research Agent Requirements

**An answer is not knowledge unless its source, method, validation, uncertainty, and responsibility remain intact.**

## Definition

An **autonomous research agent** is an AI system or agentic process that can search, retrieve, synthesize, compare, infer, cite, write, plan, recommend, or act across research materials and tools with some degree of independence.

Autonomous research agents may accelerate inquiry.

Autonomous research agents may identify patterns.

Autonomous research agents may gather sources.

Autonomous research agents may generate hypotheses.

Autonomous research agents may produce reports.

Autonomous research agents may recommend action.

Their output does not become knowledge unless source, method, validation, uncertainty, and responsibility remain intact.

## Core Rule

An autonomous research agent may produce an answer.

An autonomous research agent may gather sources.

An autonomous research agent may synthesize information.

An autonomous research agent may identify convergence.

An autonomous research agent may cite authorities.

An autonomous research agent may detect consensus.

An autonomous research agent may recommend action.

The agent’s output remains below the knowledge boundary until the claim remains traceable, testable, bounded, and accountable.

## Capability Is Not Knowledge

Capability does not equal knowledge.

A system may be able to search more quickly than a human.

A system may be able to synthesize more sources than a human.

A system may be able to compare more documents than a human.

A system may be able to generate reports faster than a human.

A system may be able to identify patterns at scale.

These capabilities may support research.

They do not complete validation.

An agent’s ability to produce answers does not mean the agent has produced knowledge.

## Access Is Not Understanding

Autonomous research agents may access large numbers of sources, tools, datasets, documents, systems, or environments.

Access does not equal understanding.

A system may retrieve a source without interpreting it correctly.

A system may summarize a source without preserving its limits.

A system may cite a source without showing that the source directly supports the claim.

A system may access data without validating the conclusion drawn from it.

Access creates possibility.

Access does not create knowledge-bearing authority.

## Synthesis Is Not Validation

Autonomous research agents may synthesize information across many sources.

Synthesis may be useful.

Synthesis may reveal patterns.

Synthesis may identify convergence.

Synthesis may organize evidence.

Synthesis may form hypotheses.

Synthesis is not validation by itself.

A synthesis must preserve:

- source status
- claim status
- evidence status
- inference status
- uncertainty status
- validation status
- responsibility status

A synthesis that hides these statuses may produce manufactured knowledge.

## Agent Output Status

Every autonomous research agent output must preserve its epistemic status.

The output may be:

- answer
- claim
- synthesis
- inference
- hypothesis
- evidence summary
- convergence finding
- authority review
- consensus review
- validation report
- recommendation
- action proposal

The status must remain visible.

The system must not allow a recommendation, summary, or report to appear as knowledge when validation is incomplete.

## Source Requirements

Autonomous research agents must preserve source integrity.

For each claim, the agent must preserve:

- where the claim came from
- what source supports it
- whether the source directly supports the claim
- whether the source is primary, secondary, interpreted, repeated, summarized, or generated
- whether the source is independent
- whether the source chain remains attached
- whether the source contains limits or uncertainty
- whether the source has been transformed
- where the source chain breaks

The agent must not cite sources as decoration.

The agent must not attach sources after the answer is formed as though provenance had been preserved.

**Source cannot be retroactively manufactured.**

## Method Requirements

Autonomous research agents must preserve method visibility.

The agent must show:

- how sources were found
- how sources were selected
- how sources were compared
- how evidence was evaluated
- how contradictions were handled
- how inference was produced
- how synthesis was formed
- how uncertainty was preserved
- how validation was attempted
- how conclusions were limited

A hidden method prevents review.

A hidden method keeps the claim below the knowledge boundary.

## Validation Requirements

Autonomous research agents must preserve validation status.

For each claim, the agent must identify:

- what has been tested
- how it has been tested
- what evidence supports it
- what evidence challenges it
- what standard applies
- what remains untested
- what uncertainty remains
- what confidence level is appropriate
- what conditions would change the claim
- who is responsible if the claim is used

Validation remains the gate.

The agent must not treat citations, convergence, authority, consensus, confidence, or fluency as validation.

## Uncertainty Requirements

Autonomous research agents must preserve uncertainty.

The agent must show:

- what is known
- what is not known
- what is inferred
- what is assumed
- what remains conditional
- what remains disputed
- what evidence is missing
- what evidence conflicts
- what confidence level is appropriate
- where the claim stops

The agent must not compress uncertainty into certainty for readability, persuasion, user comfort, institutional tone, governance language, or action pressure.

A claim that hides uncertainty is not bounded.

## Responsibility Requirements

Autonomous research agents must preserve responsibility.

The agent must identify:

- who produced the claim
- who selected the sources
- who validated the claim
- who published the claim
- who authorized use
- who applies the claim
- who is responsible for consequences
- what system carries responsibility
- what institution carries responsibility
- where responsibility cannot be assigned

Responsibility must remain attached when a claim informs action.

The more consequential the use, the stronger the responsibility requirement.

## Traceability Requirement

An autonomous research agent must make claims traceable.

A traceable agent output shows:

- the source chain
- the evidence path
- the transformation history
- the relationship between source and claim
- the relationship between claim and conclusion
- the point where inference begins
- the point where synthesis occurs
- the point where validation has or has not occurred

A claim that cannot be traced remains below the knowledge boundary.

## Testability Requirement

An autonomous research agent must make claims testable.

A testable agent output shows:

- the exact claim being evaluated
- what evidence would support it
- what evidence would challenge it
- what method applies
- what standard applies
- what remains untested
- how the claim could be reviewed
- how the claim could be challenged
- what would change the claim

A claim that cannot be tested remains below the knowledge boundary.

## Boundedness Requirement

An autonomous research agent must make claims bounded.

A bounded agent output shows:

- the scope of the claim
- the limits of the claim
- the confidence level
- the uncertainty
- the assumptions
- the conditions
- the missing evidence
- the contradictory evidence
- the domain where the claim applies
- the domain where the claim does not apply

A claim that exceeds its evidence remains below the knowledge boundary.

## Accountability Requirement

An autonomous research agent must make claims accountable.

An accountable agent output shows:

- who or what produced the output
- who or what validates the output
- who or what authorizes use
- who or what applies the output
- who or what is responsible for consequences
- where human review is required
- where institutional responsibility begins
- where system responsibility begins
- where responsibility cannot be assigned

A claim that becomes authority without accountability remains below the knowledge boundary.

## Convergence Requirements

Autonomous research agents may identify convergence.

When an agent identifies convergence, it must preserve:

- what exact claim appears to converge
- which sources support the claim
- whether the sources are independent
- whether the sources directly support the claim
- what method identified the convergence
- what evidence challenges the convergence
- what remains uncertain
- what has been validated
- what has not been validated
- who is responsible if the claim is used

Convergence is signal, not truth.

The agent must not treat convergence as validation.

## Authority Requirements

Autonomous research agents may identify authority sources.

When an agent relies on authority, it must preserve:

- who or what the authority source is
- what claim the authority source supports
- whether the authority source directly supports the claim
- whether the authority source is relevant to the domain
- whether the claim exceeds the authority source
- what method produced or supports the claim
- what evidence challenges the claim
- what remains uncertain
- what has been validated
- who is responsible if the claim is used

Authority is signal, not truth.

The agent must not treat authority as validation.

## Consensus Requirements

Autonomous research agents may identify consensus.

When an agent relies on consensus, it must preserve:

- what exact claim appears to have consensus
- where the consensus appears
- which sources support the claim
- whether the sources are independent
- what evidence challenges the consensus
- what minority evidence remains relevant
- what method identified the consensus
- what remains uncertain
- what has been validated
- who is responsible if the claim is used

Consensus is signal, not truth.

The agent must not treat consensus as validation.

## Provenance Requirements

Autonomous research agents must preserve provenance.

The agent must maintain:

- source chain
- evidence path
- transformation history
- method visibility
- validation status
- uncertainty limits
- disagreement status
- inference status
- synthesis status
- responsibility attachment
- use-context
- boundary status

The agent must not manufacture provenance after output.

Provenance must move with the claim from source through use.

## Manufactured Provenance Risk

Autonomous research agents create higher risk of manufactured provenance because they can produce research-looking output quickly and at scale.

Manufactured provenance may occur when the agent:

- forms conclusions before preserving source chain
- adds citations after the answer is produced
- selects sources to support a pre-formed conclusion
- hides source selection
- hides method steps
- hides uncertainty
- hides disagreement
- treats repeated output as confirmation
- treats authority as validation
- treats consensus as truth
- recommends action without responsibility

AI Foundations rejects manufactured provenance as a knowledge-validating act.

## Research Report Requirements

An autonomous research agent report must distinguish:

- sourced claim
- unsupported claim
- inference
- synthesis
- hypothesis
- evidence summary
- validated claim
- unresolved uncertainty
- recommendation
- action proposal

A report must not collapse these categories.

The report must preserve the difference between what was found, what was inferred, what was tested, what remains uncertain, and what is being recommended.

## Recommendation Requirements

An autonomous research agent may recommend further research, review, testing, or action.

A recommendation must preserve:

- the claim it depends on
- the validation status of that claim
- the uncertainty attached to that claim
- the consequence of the recommendation
- the responsibility attached to use
- the conditions under which the recommendation changes
- whether the recommendation is knowledge-bearing or action-bearing

A recommendation must not exceed the validation status of the claim it depends on.

## Action Requirements

An autonomous research agent must not move from claim to action without stronger validation and responsibility.

Action-bearing outputs require:

- traceable source chain
- visible method
- validated claim status
- preserved uncertainty
- attached responsibility
- consequence awareness
- use-context
- appropriate human or institutional review
- clear decision pathway
- clear accountability

The stronger the consequence, the stronger the gate.

## Human Review Requirement

Human review may be required when an autonomous research agent output informs action-bearing authority.

Human review must not be decorative.

Human review must identify:

- what was reviewed
- who reviewed it
- what standard was used
- what uncertainty remains
- what validation occurred
- what responsibility is accepted
- what action is authorized
- what action is not authorized

A human signature does not validate a claim unless review is substantive.

## Boundary Preservation Requirement

Autonomous research agents must preserve the claim-to-knowledge boundary.

The agent must keep each output in its correct epistemic status:

- answer remains answer
- claim remains claim
- synthesis remains synthesis
- inference remains inference
- hypothesis remains hypothesis
- evidence remains evidence
- validation report remains validation report
- recommendation remains recommendation
- action proposal remains action proposal

The agent must not move a claim across the boundary through presentation, confidence, speed, or scale.

## Failure Conditions

An autonomous research agent fails the AI Foundations epistemic integrity requirements when:

- source is missing
- method is hidden
- validation is incomplete
- uncertainty is compressed
- responsibility is detached
- confidence exceeds evidence
- citations do not support the claim
- convergence is treated as truth
- authority is treated as validation
- consensus is treated as proof
- synthesis hides claim status
- recommendations exceed validation
- action is proposed without accountability
- provenance is manufactured after output

Failure does not always make the output useless.

Failure keeps the output below knowledge-bearing authority.

## Preservation Conditions

An autonomous research agent preserves epistemic integrity when:

- sources remain attached
- methods remain visible
- validation remains named
- uncertainty remains present
- responsibility remains attached
- claim status remains visible
- inference remains labeled
- synthesis preserves source status
- convergence remains signal
- authority remains signal
- consensus remains signal
- validation remains gate
- provenance remains preserved
- recommendations stay within validation
- action remains accountable

Preservation allows a claim to move toward epistemic weight.

## Knowledge-Bearing Authority

An autonomous research agent’s output may carry knowledge-bearing authority only when the claim remains traceable, testable, bounded, and accountable.

Knowledge-bearing authority may inform understanding, research direction, publication, governance reasoning, institutional reasoning, or public knowledge.

Knowledge-bearing authority is earned through validation.

It is not granted by autonomy.

## Action-Bearing Authority

An autonomous research agent’s output may carry action-bearing authority only when validation and responsibility meet the consequence threshold of the use-context.

Action-bearing authority may inform decisions, interventions, policies, legal judgments, medical guidance, governance action, system behavior, or material consequences.

An agent must not carry action-bearing authority when provenance is broken, validation is incomplete, uncertainty is compressed, or responsibility is detached.

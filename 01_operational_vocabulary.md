# AI Foundations: Epistemic Integrity and Knowledge Validation

## 01 Operational Vocabulary

**An answer is not knowledge unless its source, method, validation, uncertainty, and responsibility remain intact.**

## Purpose

This file defines the operational vocabulary used by **AI Foundations: Epistemic Integrity and Knowledge Validation**.

These terms are not decorative.

They define how the claim-to-knowledge validation layer functions.

When a term in this repository determines whether an answer, claim, synthesis, inference, or hypothesis may carry epistemic weight, the term must remain defined, stable, and usable across files.

## Core Vocabulary Rule

AI Foundations uses operational terms as functional requirements.

A term is operational when it determines:

- whether a claim can be traced
- whether a method can be examined
- whether validation has occurred
- whether uncertainty remains visible
- whether responsibility remains attached
- whether a claim may cross into knowledge-bearing authority
- whether an answer must remain below the knowledge boundary

Operational terms must preserve distinction.

Operational terms must not be used as decorative authority language.

## Answer

An **answer** is a produced response.

An answer may explain, summarize, compare, recommend, cite, infer, synthesize, or propose.

An answer may be useful.

An answer may be directionally valuable.

An answer may support inquiry.

An answer is not knowledge by itself.

An answer remains below the knowledge boundary until its source, method, validation, uncertainty, and responsibility remain intact.

## Claim

A **claim** is an assertion that can be examined.

A claim may come from a human, model, agent, source, institution, dataset, report, observation, or generated output.

A claim can be tested, challenged, supported, revised, limited, rejected, or validated.

A claim remains a claim until it satisfies the conditions required for knowledge.

## Knowledge

**Knowledge** is a validated claim whose source, method, uncertainty, and responsibility remain attached through use.

Knowledge requires more than fluency, confidence, citation, repetition, authority, or consensus.

Knowledge carries epistemic weight only when the claim remains traceable, testable, bounded, and accountable.

## Source

A **source** is the origin point, record, data, observation, evidence, author, system, institution, or material from which a claim is derived.

Source includes where the claim came from and what supports it.

A source must remain attached to the claim in a way that allows the claim to be traced.

A source cannot be retroactively manufactured.

## Method

A **method** is the process used to gather, compare, infer, test, synthesize, produce, validate, or apply a claim.

Method includes how the claim was formed, how evidence was handled, how comparisons were made, and how conclusions were reached.

A method must remain visible enough for the claim to be examined.

A hidden method weakens the claim’s ability to carry knowledge-bearing authority.

## Validation

**Validation** is the process by which a claim is tested against evidence, method, reality, reproducibility, or appropriate domain standards.

Validation is the gate between claim and knowledge.

Validation determines whether a claim has earned epistemic weight.

Validation is not completed by authority, consensus, convergence, confidence, or presentation.

## Uncertainty

**Uncertainty** is the visible preservation of what remains unresolved, untested, unknown, limited, conditional, inferred, or open.

Uncertainty includes limits, confidence level, assumptions, missing evidence, disputed evidence, unresolved questions, and conditions under which the claim may change.

Uncertainty must remain attached to the claim.

When uncertainty is compressed into confidence, the claim loses boundary integrity.

## Responsibility

**Responsibility** is the attachment of an actor, system, institution, human, validator, publisher, decision-maker, or user to the production, validation, publication, application, or consequence of a claim.

Responsibility names who stands behind the use of the claim.

Responsibility must remain attached when a claim informs action, governance, publication, scientific conclusion, legal judgment, medical guidance, institutional decision, or public authority.

## Traceable

A claim is **traceable** when its origin, source chain, evidence path, and transformation history can be followed.

Traceability preserves:

- where the claim came from
- what source supports it
- how the claim moved from source into output
- what was quoted
- what was summarized
- what was inferred
- what was generated
- what transformations occurred
- where the source chain remains intact
- where the source chain breaks

A claim that cannot be traced cannot cross the knowledge boundary.

## Testable

A claim is **testable** when it can be examined against evidence, method, reality, reproducibility, or appropriate domain standards.

Testability requires the claim to be specific enough to evaluate.

A testable claim can be checked, challenged, measured, compared, reproduced, reviewed, or falsified by the relevant method.

A claim that cannot be tested remains below the knowledge boundary.

## Bounded

A claim is **bounded** when its scope, limits, confidence, conditions, and uncertainty remain visible.

A bounded claim preserves:

- what is known
- what is not known
- what is inferred
- what is assumed
- what is uncertain
- what is conditional
- what evidence supports it
- what evidence does not support it
- where the claim stops

A claim that exceeds its evidence is not bounded.

## Accountable

A claim is **accountable** when responsibility remains attached to its production, validation, publication, use, decision, or consequence.

Accountability preserves:

- who produced the claim
- who validated the claim
- who published the claim
- who authorized use
- who applied the claim
- who is responsible for consequences
- what system or institution carries responsibility
- where responsibility cannot be assigned

A claim that becomes authority without responsibility is not accountable.

## Convergence

**Convergence** is the appearance of alignment across multiple sources, systems, observations, reports, outputs, or methods.

Convergence may show that more than one source points toward a similar claim.

Convergence creates a signal for further examination.

Convergence does not complete validation.

Convergence is signal, not truth.

## Authority

**Authority** is the weight given to a source, institution, expert, system, field, publication, dataset, or method because of its role, expertise, record, status, or domain relevance.

Authority may provide context.

Authority may guide attention.

Authority may increase the need for careful examination.

Authority does not make a claim true by itself.

Authority is signal, not truth.

## Consensus

**Consensus** is the appearance of broad agreement across a field, institution, group, public record, source set, or repeated claim environment.

Consensus may show what is widely accepted, widely repeated, or currently dominant.

Consensus may guide review.

Consensus does not replace testing.

Consensus is signal, not truth.

## Signal

A **signal** is an indication that a claim deserves attention, examination, testing, or review.

A signal may come from convergence, authority, consensus, anomaly, pattern, repetition, contradiction, or evidence.

A signal is not final truth.

A signal directs inquiry.

## Truth

**Truth** is not granted by repetition, authority, consensus, fluency, confidence, or presentation.

Within this repository, truth is approached through intact source, visible method, preserved uncertainty, validation under appropriate standards, and attached responsibility.

AI Foundations does not treat majority agreement as truth.

AI Foundations does not treat authority status as truth.

AI Foundations does not treat generated coherence as truth.

## Gate

A **gate** is a validation threshold that a claim must satisfy before it may move into a higher epistemic status.

Validation is the gate between claim and knowledge.

A gate preserves the boundary between output and authority.

A gate prevents uncertainty from becoming authority before the claim’s conditions remain intact.

## Boundary

A **boundary** is the distinction between one epistemic status and another.

The claim-to-knowledge boundary separates:

- answer from knowledge
- claim from validated claim
- synthesis from proof
- inference from evidence
- convergence from truth
- authority from validation
- consensus from knowledge
- confidence from certainty
- production from responsibility

The boundary preserves the correct status of the claim.

## Boundary Crossing

**Boundary crossing** is the movement of a claim from one epistemic status into another.

A valid boundary crossing occurs when the claim satisfies the requirements of the next status.

A manufactured boundary crossing occurs when presentation, authority language, citation-like structure, consensus pressure, or institutional tone moves a claim into knowledge status without intact validation.

AI Foundations requires boundary crossing to be earned through source, method, validation, uncertainty, and responsibility.

## Provenance

**Provenance** is the preserved record of where a claim came from, how it moved, how it changed, what supported it, and how it was used.

Provenance includes source chain, evidence path, transformation history, method visibility, validation status, and responsibility attachment.

Provenance allows the claim to remain traceable.

## Manufactured Provenance

**Manufactured provenance** occurs when an answer, claim, output, or report is produced first and later wrapped in source-like, citation-like, method-like, or validation-like language as though provenance had been preserved.

Manufactured provenance creates the appearance of traceability without preserving the actual claim chain.

AI Foundations rejects manufactured provenance as a knowledge-validating act.

**Source cannot be retroactively manufactured.**

## Inference

An **inference** is a conclusion drawn from available information.

An inference may be reasonable.

An inference may be useful.

An inference may guide inquiry.

An inference is not evidence by itself.

An inference must remain labeled as inference until it is validated.

## Synthesis

A **synthesis** is a structured combination of sources, findings, patterns, claims, or observations.

A synthesis may organize information.

A synthesis may reveal relationships.

A synthesis may identify convergence or contradiction.

A synthesis is not validation by itself.

A synthesis must preserve the source status, uncertainty, and method behind each included claim.

## Hypothesis

A **hypothesis** is a testable proposal.

A hypothesis may emerge from answer production, inference, synthesis, anomaly, pattern recognition, or research direction.

A hypothesis is valuable because it can be tested.

A hypothesis is not knowledge until validation occurs.

## Evidence

**Evidence** is material, observation, data, record, result, source, measurement, or tested finding that supports or challenges a claim.

Evidence must remain connected to the claim it supports.

Evidence must be examined through method.

Evidence must not be replaced by citation appearance, authority language, or repeated output.

## Confidence

**Confidence** is an expressed estimate of support, reliability, probability, or strength.

Confidence may communicate how strongly a claim appears to be supported.

Confidence does not replace source.

Confidence does not replace method.

Confidence does not replace validation.

Confidence must remain bounded by uncertainty.

## Certainty

**Certainty** is the presentation of a claim as settled.

AI Foundations treats certainty as a high-risk epistemic status when source, method, validation, uncertainty, and responsibility are not intact.

A system must not present certainty where the claim remains unresolved, inferred, conditional, untested, or responsibility-detached.

## Epistemic Weight

**Epistemic weight** is the degree of knowledge-bearing strength a claim may carry after review.

A claim gains epistemic weight when source, method, validation, uncertainty, and responsibility remain intact.

Epistemic weight is earned through validation.

Epistemic weight is not granted by tone, polish, repetition, authority, or consensus.

## Knowledge-Bearing Authority

**Knowledge-bearing authority** is the status a claim carries when it is treated as reliable enough to inform understanding, publication, governance, institutional reasoning, research direction, or public knowledge.

Knowledge-bearing authority requires intact source, method, validation, uncertainty, and responsibility.

A claim must not carry knowledge-bearing authority before it crosses the claim-to-knowledge boundary.

## Action-Bearing Authority

**Action-bearing authority** is the status a claim carries when it is used to support decisions, interventions, policies, legal judgments, medical guidance, governance action, system behavior, or material consequences.

Action-bearing authority requires a higher responsibility threshold because the claim is connected to consequence.

A claim must not carry action-bearing authority when responsibility is detached or validation is incomplete.

## Autonomous Research Agent

An **autonomous research agent** is an AI system or agentic process that can search, retrieve, synthesize, compare, infer, cite, write, plan, recommend, or act across research materials and tools with some degree of independence.

An autonomous research agent may accelerate inquiry.

An autonomous research agent may produce useful output.

An autonomous research agent may identify sources, patterns, and hypotheses.

An autonomous research agent’s output remains below the knowledge boundary until the claim remains traceable, testable, bounded, and accountable.

## Operational Vocabulary Requirement

Any term used as a validation condition, boundary condition, gate condition, authority condition, or responsibility condition must remain defined.

If a term determines whether a claim may move from answer to knowledge, it belongs to the operational vocabulary.

If a term is used only as prose and does not determine claim status, it may remain decorative.

AI Foundations requires operational vocabulary to stay visible so that the system cannot hide epistemic movement inside undefined language.

# AI Foundations: Epistemic Integrity and Knowledge Validation

## 10 Manufactured Provenance

**An answer is not knowledge unless its source, method, validation, uncertainty, and responsibility remain intact.**

## Definition

**Manufactured provenance** occurs when an answer, claim, output, report, synthesis, inference, or recommendation is produced first and later wrapped in source-like, citation-like, method-like, validation-like, or authority-like language as though the claim chain had remained intact.

Manufactured provenance creates the appearance of traceability without preserving the actual source chain.

Manufactured provenance creates the appearance of validation without preserving the actual validation process.

Manufactured provenance creates the appearance of knowledge without preserving source, method, uncertainty, and responsibility.

AI Foundations rejects manufactured provenance as a knowledge-validating act.

**Source cannot be retroactively manufactured.**

## Core Rule

A claim must carry its source chain through the process that produces, presents, validates, and applies it.

A system may not produce an answer first and later attach source-like, method-like, or validation-like structure as though provenance had been preserved.

**Source cannot be retroactively manufactured.**

## What Provenance Preserves

Provenance is the preserved record of where a claim came from, how it moved, how it changed, what supported it, and how it was used.

Provenance preserves:

- source chain
- evidence path
- transformation history
- method visibility
- validation status
- uncertainty limits
- responsibility attachment
- use-context
- publication status
- decision pathway

Provenance allows the claim to remain traceable.

## What Manufactured Provenance Does

Manufactured provenance imitates provenance.

It may make an output appear:

- sourced
- cited
- validated
- reviewed
- authoritative
- institutionally grounded
- methodologically sound
- responsibly produced
- knowledge-bearing
- action-ready

The appearance may be clean.

The claim chain may still be broken.

Manufactured provenance replaces preserved structure with presentation.

## Manufactured Provenance and Source

Manufactured provenance breaks source integrity when a claim is made first and source language is added after the fact.

This may happen when:

- citations are added after the answer is formed
- sources are selected to support a pre-formed conclusion
- a source is attached to a claim it does not support
- a weak source is presented as strong support
- a generated claim is made to appear sourced
- a summary is treated as primary source
- repeated claims are treated as independent sourcing
- source context is removed
- a source chain is hidden or broken

A source must support the claim directly.

A source must not be used as decoration.

## Manufactured Provenance and Method

Manufactured provenance breaks method integrity when a system presents method-like language without a reviewable process.

This may happen when:

- the system says research was performed without showing how
- source selection is hidden
- comparison method is hidden
- exclusion of contradictory evidence is hidden
- inference steps are hidden
- synthesis appears complete without method visibility
- validation language appears without testing
- confidence is produced without explanation
- summary compression hides the process

Method-like language is not method.

A claim cannot be examined when its method is hidden.

## Manufactured Provenance and Validation

Manufactured provenance breaks validation integrity when an output appears tested without preserving the actual validation process.

This may happen when:

- citations are treated as validation
- authority is treated as validation
- consensus is treated as validation
- convergence is treated as validation
- confidence scores are treated as validation
- institutional tone is treated as validation
- peer-like language is used without review
- testing is implied but not shown
- untested claims are presented as established

Validation is the gate between claim and knowledge.

Manufactured validation cannot open that gate.

## Manufactured Provenance and Uncertainty

Manufactured provenance breaks uncertainty integrity when limits are removed so the claim appears stronger than it is.

This may happen when:

- uncertainty is removed for readability
- unresolved conditions are hidden
- missing evidence is omitted
- conflicting evidence is compressed
- inference is presented as fact
- possibility is presented as conclusion
- confidence is presented as certainty
- partial evidence is presented as complete knowledge
- a bounded claim is expanded beyond its support

A claim without preserved uncertainty cannot remain bounded.

## Manufactured Provenance and Responsibility

Manufactured provenance breaks responsibility integrity when a claim becomes authority without an accountable actor, system, validator, publisher, decision-maker, or use-context.

This may happen when:

- passive voice hides responsibility
- automation hides the decision path
- system output replaces accountable judgment
- institutional tone hides ownership
- publication status is unclear
- validation responsibility is unnamed
- use responsibility is unnamed
- action is recommended without accountable use-context
- consequences are detached from the claim

A claim without responsibility cannot carry action-bearing authority.

## Manufactured Traceability

Manufactured traceability occurs when a claim appears traceable but the actual source chain cannot be followed.

This may happen through:

- citation-like formatting
- source lists without claim mapping
- links that do not support the claim
- references added after the answer is formed
- vague attribution
- repeated summaries
- generated support language
- missing transformation history

Manufactured traceability does not satisfy the traceability requirement.

A claim must be traceable through preserved source chain, not through citation appearance.

## Manufactured Testability

Manufactured testability occurs when a claim appears testable but cannot actually be examined against evidence, method, reality, reproducibility, or appropriate domain standards.

This may happen through:

- vague claims framed as conclusions
- method-like language without method
- implied testing without test record
- confidence scores without validation
- broad conclusions without evaluable conditions
- claims that cannot be challenged by relevant method
- output that appears scientific, legal, medical, or governance-ready without reviewable testing

Manufactured testability does not satisfy the testability requirement.

A claim must be testable through actual reviewable structure.

## Manufactured Boundedness

Manufactured boundedness occurs when a claim appears limited but the limits are generic, incomplete, detached, or decorative.

This may happen through:

- generic disclaimers
- vague caution language
- uncertainty mentioned but not attached to the claim
- limits placed outside the conclusion
- confidence language that exceeds evidence
- known unknowns omitted from the claim
- exceptions hidden from the summary
- action language stronger than validation status

Manufactured boundedness does not satisfy the boundedness requirement.

A claim must preserve its actual scope, limits, uncertainty, and conditions.

## Manufactured Accountability

Manufactured accountability occurs when a claim appears responsible but no accountable actor, system, institution, validator, publisher, decision-maker, or use-context remains attached.

This may happen through:

- governance language without named responsibility
- institutional tone without ownership
- model output without responsible use-context
- collective language that hides actors
- unclear publication status
- unclear validation status
- unclear decision authority
- unclear consequence responsibility

Manufactured accountability does not satisfy the accountability requirement.

A claim must preserve responsibility through use.

## Manufactured Authority

Manufactured authority occurs when presentation makes a claim appear authoritative without preserving source, method, validation, uncertainty, or responsibility.

This may happen through:

- polished language
- institutional tone
- expert-like phrasing
- citation appearance
- confidence language
- consensus language
- governance language
- scientific language
- legal language
- medical language

Authority-like output is not authority.

Authority does not replace validation.

## Manufactured Consensus

Manufactured consensus occurs when a system creates the appearance of broad agreement without preserving source independence, method visibility, disagreement, uncertainty, or validation status.

This may happen when:

- only agreeing sources are selected
- contradictory evidence is ignored
- repetition is counted as independent support
- public repetition is treated as validation
- generated outputs reinforce generated outputs
- weak sources are counted as consensus
- disagreement is compressed
- uncertainty is removed

Consensus is signal, not truth.

Manufactured consensus is not validation.

## Manufactured Convergence

Manufactured convergence occurs when a system creates the appearance of alignment without preserving source chain, source independence, method, validation status, uncertainty, or responsibility.

This may happen when:

- sources share the same upstream error
- summaries repeat one another
- generated outputs repeat generated outputs
- similarity is overstated
- contradiction is removed
- evidence paths are not separated
- source status is hidden
- convergence is presented without method

Convergence is signal, not truth.

Manufactured convergence is not validation.

## Manufactured Knowledge

Manufactured knowledge occurs when output is treated as knowledge because it appears complete, sourced, validated, authoritative, repeated, or institutionally clean.

Manufactured knowledge may occur when:

- answer production is mistaken for knowledge validation
- source appearance is mistaken for source integrity
- method language is mistaken for method visibility
- citation appearance is mistaken for validation
- confidence is mistaken for certainty
- consensus is mistaken for truth
- authority is mistaken for validation
- output polish is mistaken for epistemic weight
- responsibility is detached from use

AI Foundations rejects manufactured knowledge.

A claim becomes knowledge only when its source, method, validation, uncertainty, and responsibility remain intact.

## Manufactured Boundary Crossing

Manufactured boundary crossing occurs when a claim is moved into knowledge-bearing authority through presentation rather than validation.

This may happen when:

- an answer looks complete
- a report looks institutional
- a source list looks sufficient
- a conclusion looks confident
- a synthesis looks comprehensive
- a claim looks widely accepted
- a recommendation looks actionable
- an agent output looks research-grade

A claim may not cross the claim-to-knowledge boundary because it looks ready.

The claim crosses only when it remains traceable, testable, bounded, and accountable.

## Manufactured Provenance in Autonomous Research Agents

Autonomous research agents increase the risk of manufactured provenance.

An autonomous research agent may search, retrieve, compare, synthesize, infer, cite, write, plan, recommend, or act at scale.

This can produce outputs that appear sourced and validated before the claim chain is intact.

An autonomous research agent must not:

- produce conclusions first and source them later
- hide source selection
- hide method steps
- hide uncertainty
- hide disagreement
- treat repeated output as confirmation
- treat authority as validation
- treat consensus as truth
- recommend action without responsibility
- present agentic output as knowledge without intact validation

The more autonomous the agent becomes, the more important provenance preservation becomes.

## Detection Questions

To detect manufactured provenance, a system should ask:

1. Was the claim formed before the source chain was preserved?
2. Does each source directly support the claim?
3. Can the path from source to output be followed?
4. Is the method visible?
5. Was validation actually performed?
6. What uncertainty remains attached?
7. What evidence challenges the claim?
8. Are sources independent?
9. Is confidence bounded by evidence?
10. Who is responsible if the claim is used?
11. Has presentation replaced preserved structure?
12. Has the claim crossed a boundary through language rather than validation?

These questions prevent provenance from becoming decorative.

## Provenance Preservation Requirements

A system preserves provenance when it maintains:

- source chain
- evidence path
- transformation history
- method visibility
- validation status
- uncertainty limits
- disagreement status
- inference status
- synthesis status
- responsibility attachment
- use-context
- boundary status

The claim must remain connected to its origin, process, validation, limits, and responsibility.

## Provenance Failure Conditions

Provenance fails when:

- the source chain breaks
- the method is hidden
- validation is implied but not shown
- uncertainty is compressed
- responsibility is detached
- citations do not support the claim
- conclusions exceed sources
- synthesis hides claim status
- inference is presented as fact
- consensus is treated as proof
- authority is treated as validation
- output is polished into authority

When provenance fails, the claim remains below the knowledge boundary.

## Knowledge-Bearing Authority

A claim may carry knowledge-bearing authority only when provenance remains intact through validation.

Knowledge-bearing authority requires:

- traceable source chain
- visible method
- actual validation
- preserved uncertainty
- attached responsibility
- bounded scope
- reviewable claim status

A claim with manufactured provenance may not carry knowledge-bearing authority.

## Action-Bearing Authority

A claim may carry action-bearing authority only when provenance remains intact and responsibility remains attached to consequence.

Action-bearing authority requires stronger validation because the claim may inform decisions, interventions, policies, legal judgments, medical guidance, governance action, system behavior, or material consequences.

A claim with manufactured provenance may not carry action-bearing authority.

## Core Standard

The core standard of this file is:

**Provenance must be preserved through the claim chain, not manufactured after output.**
